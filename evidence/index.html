EVIDENCE-LED SEO

Evidence, Not Ranking Promises

I document SEO work with the source, time period, context and limitations visible. That includes my own website measurements, practical audits and authorized client evidence when it becomes available. Demonstration material is kept separate from first-party results, and I do not publish invented traffic charts, fabricated reviews or unsupported ranking claims.

My approach is simple: proof over promises. The About page explains the broader principles behind that approach through its existing Proof Over Promises and Credibility Through Evidence sections. This page focuses on how the evidence itself is documented, measured and presented.

How I Document SEO Evidence

A useful SEO report should let someone understand what was measured, where the information came from, when it was observed and what the measurement does not prove. I therefore separate implementation findings from performance outcomes and avoid turning a short-term snapshot into a claim about rankings, traffic, leads or revenue.

When first-party or authorized evidence becomes available, I will identify the relevant property or project, date range, metric definition, important implementation changes and known limitations. This makes the record easier to review and harder to misinterpret.

What Counts as Evidence

  • First-party measurements from the website or an authorized analytics property.
  • Documented technical findings that can be reproduced or independently checked.
  • Authorized client evidence with the relevant project context and permission to publish.
  • Clearly labeled demonstrations used to explain a reporting method rather than claim performance.

What does not count: invented metrics, anonymous success stories, copied screenshots, fabricated reviews, unsupported ranking claims or third-party data presented as my own.

Trust note: Metrics are time-bound and context-dependent. Search Console clicks, Analytics sessions, engagement metrics and PageSpeed diagnostics measure different things. A performance snapshot is not a guarantee of rankings, traffic, leads or revenue.
