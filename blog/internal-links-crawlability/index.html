Internal Links and Crawlability: Build a Website Google Can Discover

Niaz Mahmud Minhaz6 min readTechnical workflow

Internal links are part of your site's information architecture, not decoration. Google says links help it find new pages and understand relevance, and it recommends making important pages reachable through real <a href> links with descriptive anchor text.

Google: Link best practices ↗

1. Every important page needs a discoverable path

Google's developer guidance says every page you care about should be reachable through a link from another findable page. For a static site, that means your homepage, service hubs, category pages, articles and important supporting URLs should be connected in the visible HTML architecture.

A sitemap helps Google discover URLs, but it should not be your only discovery mechanism. Think of the sitemap as a map and internal links as the roads users and crawlers actually travel.

2. Use real anchor elements

A crawlable link is normally an anchor element with an href. JavaScript click handlers can improve interactions, but they should not replace the underlying link when navigation matters for search discovery.

Static-site rule: if a page matters for SEO, link to it with a normal HTML anchor somewhere else on the site.

3. Write descriptive anchor text

“Click here” tells very little. “Technical SEO audit” or “keyword research guide” tells both users and search engines what they will find. Google recommends using anchor text that gives context about the linked page.

4. Build thematic clusters

A useful structure for an SEO consultancy site is a clear hierarchy: the main SEO service hub connects to specialized services; research content connects to those services where relevant; case studies and FAQs reinforce the same concepts from an evidence or decision-making angle.

This is where your existing Insights architecture becomes useful. A keyword research guide can link to On-Page SEO and content strategy. An AI search article can link to AI Search SEO, AEO and GEO. The links should exist because the reader needs them, not because you want to force every page into every other page.

5. Think about orphan pages

An orphan page has no meaningful internal links pointing to it. Even if it appears in a sitemap, it can be harder for users to discover and for crawlers to understand in the context of the site. A simple crawl or internal-link audit should identify URLs with weak or missing internal paths.

6. Connect commercial and informational intent

Strong internal linking makes the site useful at multiple stages of the search journey. A reader can move from a practical guide to the relevant service, then to evidence, then to a consultation. That is better architecture than isolating informational and commercial content in separate silos with no useful bridges.

Internal linking checklist

  • Important URLs are reachable through normal HTML links.
  • Anchor text describes the destination clearly.
  • New articles link to older, relevant resources.
  • Service pages link to supporting research and evidence.
  • No critical page is isolated or orphaned.
  • Links serve a user need and preserve a logical topical path.

Bottom line

Crawlability is partly a technical problem and partly an information-architecture problem. A website that is easy to navigate for a human is often easier for search systems to understand as well. Build the link graph deliberately, keep it useful and let the site's structure communicate what matters.
