Inventory important URLs first

Export important pages, organic landing pages, conversion paths and relevant historical URLs before changing the site. This creates a baseline and exposes pages that should not disappear by accident.

Preserve the inventory and implementation notes so post-launch problems can be traced back to a specific change.

Map old URLs to relevant new destinations

Create a specific old-to-new mapping. Avoid redirecting every old URL to the homepage. Where an equivalent does not exist, decide deliberately whether to retire, retain or redirect the page.

Test redirect chains, loops, irrelevant destinations and broken URLs before launch.

Protect canonicals, internal links and sitemaps

The new site should link to the new URLs, use its intended canonical signals and expose preferred URLs in the sitemap. Robots directives should reflect the production environment.

Crawl representative templates before release when possible and test important page types rather than one sample URL.

Verify and monitor after launch

Check status codes, redirects, indexability, canonicals, sitemaps, Search Console, analytics, structured data and important templates immediately after deployment.

Then compare clicks, impressions, indexed pages, landing pages and conversions with the baseline. A migration is not finished when the new site goes live.

Practical checklist

  • Export URLs and baseline data
  • Create old-to-new mappings
  • Test redirects and canonical signals
  • Update internal links and sitemaps
  • Verify tracking and indexation
  • Monitor post-launch performance

Questions people usually ask

Should URLs change during a redesign?

Avoid unnecessary URL changes. When a change is justified, build and test the redirect plan before launch.

Can migration affect organic traffic?

Changed URLs can take time to be processed. Good mappings, redirects and monitoring reduce avoidable problems.

How long should I monitor?

Monitor closely after launch and continue until indexing and organic performance are stable enough to compare with the baseline.
