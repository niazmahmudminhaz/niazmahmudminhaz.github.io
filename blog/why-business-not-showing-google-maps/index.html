First separate absence from low ranking

A business can be missing because the profile is not eligible, not verified or not visible to the public. That is a different problem from ranking lower for a particular query or location.

Test a small, defined set of meaningful searches and record the location and date. One personalized result is not enough to diagnose a whole profile.

Diagnose relevance, distance and prominence

Google describes local results mainly through relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance asks how well the profile matches the search. Distance reflects the geographic relationship. Prominence reflects how established and well-known the business appears.

These factors interact. Improving one does not guarantee a fixed position because local results are query- and location-dependent.

Align the profile with the website

The Business Profile, website, local pages and external references should communicate the same accurate business identity and service story. A profile that says one thing while the website says another can create ambiguity.

Avoid fake locations, keyword-stuffed names and manufactured reviews. Sustainable local SEO starts with real-world accuracy and useful customer information.

Use repeatable testing and business metrics

Run defined tests, monitor profile visibility and connect the result to website actions, calls, enquiries or bookings where possible. Search Console and Analytics can help with the website side.

The goal is not to obsess over one Maps screenshot. The goal is to improve the chance that the right customers can discover and act on the business.

Practical checklist

  • Check eligibility and verification
  • Verify categories and business details
  • Review site/profile consistency
  • Test meaningful queries from defined locations
  • Assess genuine reputation signals
  • Track qualified actions

Questions people usually ask

Can I pay Google for a better Maps position?

Google states that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

Why can a competitor appear while I do not?

The competitor may be a stronger relevance, distance or prominence match for that particular search.

Should I create a page for every neighbourhood?

Only when the page serves a real, distinct user need and adds useful local information.
