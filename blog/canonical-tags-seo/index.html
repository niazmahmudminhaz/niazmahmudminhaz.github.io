Understand the canonical signal

Large sites can expose similar content through parameters, filters, old URL paths or duplicate templates. A canonical signal communicates which URL is preferred to represent the content group.

The preferred URL should genuinely represent the group. Canonicalization is part of a broader URL strategy, not a way to hide every technical problem.

Canonical is a hint, not a command

Google documents canonicalization as a hint and may select a different URL. Consistency across page-level canonicals, internal links and sitemaps therefore matters.

When multiple signals point to the same representative URL, the site's preference is easier to understand.

Use redirects when URLs move

A redirect and a canonical solve different problems. When users should be sent from an old URL to a new location, use the appropriate permanent redirect rather than relying on a canonical tag.

During migrations, map old URLs to the most relevant destinations instead of sending everything to the homepage.

Audit canonicals in context

Inspect status codes, internal links, sitemap entries, page content and Search Console indexing information together. If Google chooses a different canonical, investigate the signal conflict or content relationship.

Do not canonicalize unrelated pages merely because they share words. The relationship should be real.

Practical checklist

  • Inventory duplicate URL groups
  • Choose a genuine representative
  • Use coherent self-referencing signals
  • Align internal links and sitemaps
  • Use redirects for permanent moves
  • Monitor selected canonicals

Questions people usually ask

Should every page have a canonical?

A self-referencing canonical is common, but the final implementation should follow the site's URL strategy and avoid conflict.

Can canonical tags remove pages from search?

They can influence the representative URL, but they are not a universal deindexing mechanism.

Why did Google choose another canonical?

Google may determine another URL is a better representative from the content and available signals.
