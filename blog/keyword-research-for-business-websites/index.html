Start with the business model

List actual products, services, customer problems, audiences and buying stages before opening a keyword tool. This keeps the research connected to what the business can genuinely satisfy.

Search volume is useful context, but a high-volume term with poor business relevance can be less useful than a smaller query that clearly represents a valuable customer need.

Classify intent before building a keyword list

Separate informational, commercial, transactional, navigational and local intent. Then inspect live results to see which page types are being served.

This prevents a common mistake: putting every keyword on one service page. Close variants can share a page when intent is the same, while different tasks deserve different destinations.

Map topics to URLs and use competitors carefully

Build a page map with query theme, intent, destination URL, supporting questions and business goal. Use competitor pages to identify recurring subtopics and information gaps, not to copy their structure word for word.

The information gain opportunity is usually in the explanation, example, evidence or experience you can add that the current results do not explain well.

Prioritize and refine with first-party evidence

Consider relevance, competition, opportunity, effort and conversion potential. After publication, Search Console can reveal the real query variations and page relationships that appeared.

Keyword research should remain a living system. Revisit important maps after new products, services, market changes and meaningful search-data shifts.

Practical checklist

  • Define offerings and audiences
  • Collect customer-language seed topics
  • Classify intent from live results
  • Map themes to page types
  • Use competitors to identify information gaps
  • Refine with Search Console data

Questions people usually ask

How many keywords should one page target?

There is no fixed number. One page should cover a coherent topic and intent, including close variants naturally.

Should I target high-volume keywords first?

Not always. Relevance, intent, competition, business value and feasibility all matter.

Can Search Console help keyword research?

Yes. It reveals the queries and pages already receiving impressions and clicks, making it useful for refinement.
