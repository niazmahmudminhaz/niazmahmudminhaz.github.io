SEO Glossary: The Terms Behind the Search System

A practical reference for clients, marketers and SEO learners. Definitions are written for implementation rather than jargon collecting.

Technical SEO

These terms are easier to apply when connected to a real audit. See the Technical SEO service for the practical consulting context behind crawlability, indexability and canonicalization.

Crawlability

How easily important URLs can be discovered and accessed by search engine crawlers.

Indexability

Whether a page is eligible to appear in a search index after technical and content evaluation.

Canonical URL

The preferred URL for a group of duplicate or near-duplicate pages.

Rendering

The process through which a browser or search system processes a page's resources and output.

Structured Data

Machine-readable markup that helps describe page entities, properties and relationships.

Content and Search

Search Intent

The underlying task a user is trying to complete through a query.

Semantic SEO

A relevance approach that focuses on concepts, relationships, entities and comprehensive coverage rather than isolated keywords.

E-E-A-T

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trust, concepts used in Google's quality evaluation guidance.

Modern Search

AEO

Answer Engine Optimization, focused on clear direct answers plus the supporting context needed to understand them.

GEO

Generative Engine Optimization, a term commonly used for improving information clarity and usefulness in generative search experiences.

Authority

Backlink

A link from another website to your website. Quality depends on relevance, context and the referring source, not just quantity.

Turn the glossary into a learning system

A glossary works best when each definition gives the reader enough context to understand why the term matters. Use short definitions for simple concepts and link complex terms to deeper field guides.

Related terms should connect naturally. For example, canonicalization can lead to URL architecture and migration, while search intent can lead to keyword research and on-page SEO.

A glossary should not replace practical guides

Definitions answer “what is this?” Practical guides answer “how do I apply it?” Keep the glossary as a reference hub and let deeper articles handle implementation, examples and trade-offs.

This also reduces duplicate content between the two existing glossary pages. Over time, consolidate repeated definitions into one authoritative entry and link to supporting guides.

Glossary maintenance rules

Review definitions when Google or platform documentation changes. Remove outdated terminology, preserve useful redirects when URLs are consolidated and keep internal links reciprocal and descriptive.

A smaller, maintained glossary is more useful than a giant list that nobody can navigate.

Glossary quality checklist

  • Define the term in plain language
  • Explain why it matters
  • Link to a deeper practical guide
  • Consolidate duplicate definitions
  • Review outdated terminology
  • Keep the navigation easy to scan
