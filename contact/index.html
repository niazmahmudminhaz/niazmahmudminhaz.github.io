START WITH THE BUSINESS QUESTION

A stronger SEO conversation starts with context, not jargon.

Search visibility is usually the visible part of a bigger business problem. A page may be missing from search because its intent is unclear, because the site is difficult for search engines to crawl, because the local signals are weak, because the content does not answer the right need, or because the measurement setup is not showing the full picture. Those situations can look similar from the outside, but they should not be treated as the same SEO task.

That is why this contact page is deliberately simple. You can describe the situation in normal language and provide the evidence you already have. From there, the work can be framed around the right SEO question instead of starting with a generic checklist.

When the issue is technical

Tell me what changed, which templates or pages are affected, and whether the problem involves crawling, indexing, speed, redirects, canonicalization, structured data, JavaScript or a migration. You do not need to know the correct diagnosis in advance. The useful information is what you can observe and when it started.

When the issue is visibility

Share the search terms, services, products, locations or pages that matter most. This helps separate a broad ranking concern from a more specific question around search intent, on-page relevance, local visibility, content coverage or competition.

When traffic or leads change

A drop in clicks does not automatically mean the same thing as a drop in demand. Dates, affected pages, search queries, device mix, countries and conversion patterns can all change the interpretation. When you have that context, the discussion can begin with evidence rather than assumptions.

When you are planning something new

A new website, service area, product range, content program or market can create SEO decisions before launch. Early consultation can be useful for information architecture, keyword research, internal linking, local or international targeting, technical requirements and measurement planning.

HOW I THINK THROUGH SEO PROBLEMS

Look at the signal. Understand the cause. Choose the next move.

A useful SEO process is not just a list of recommendations. It is a sequence of decisions that connects search behaviour, website structure, content, technical conditions and business priorities.

01

Define the search question

What does the audience need, what is the business trying to achieve, and which search behaviour actually matters? The answer sets the direction for the rest of the investigation.

02

Separate signal from assumption

Ranking changes, indexing reports, analytics data, page behaviour, competitor patterns and search results can provide different clues. They should be interpreted together rather than treated as isolated facts.

03

Prioritize the work

Not every SEO issue deserves the same effort. The useful question is which change is relevant to the business goal, feasible for the current site and worth testing first.

SEO is connected

Technical SEO can affect whether content is discovered. Information architecture can affect internal links and intent. Content quality can affect relevance. Local signals can affect location-based discovery. Analytics can affect how the outcome is measured. Good decisions respect those connections.

Not every problem needs more pages

More URLs are not automatically better. Sometimes the better move is to improve an existing page, consolidate overlapping content, fix a technical constraint, clarify the search intent, strengthen an important internal path or stop a tactic that is creating noise.

WHAT A GOOD FIRST MESSAGE CAN REVEAL

Useful SEO questions are often more specific than “How do I rank higher?”

The more clearly the problem is described, the easier it becomes to identify whether the next step is a consultation, an audit, a focused service or further investigation.

Examples of useful questions

  • Why are important pages not appearing for the searches we actually care about?
  • Why did organic clicks change after our redesign or migration?
  • Which pages should target a particular search intent, and which should not?
  • Is our local visibility limited by the website, Google Business Profile, relevance, location or something else?
  • Which technical issues should be fixed before we invest more in content?
  • Do our ecommerce categories and product pages reflect how people search?
  • How should we approach search visibility as AI-generated search experiences change the journey?

Questions I will not force into a package

Sometimes the evidence does not support a large SEO engagement yet. A page may need a clearer business proposition, a tracking correction, a product-market change, a development fix or simply more time for a meaningful measurement window.

The contact process is designed to make that distinction easier. A good SEO recommendation should explain what is known, what is uncertain and what should be tested next.

MAKE YOUR MESSAGE USEFUL

What should you include in your first SEO enquiry?

You do not need to write a long brief. A few specific details can make the first conversation much more efficient and help avoid generic SEO advice.

Helpful details

  • Your website URL and the main business, service or product you care about.
  • Your target country, city or audience, especially for local or international SEO.
  • The outcome you are trying to improve: visibility, qualified traffic, enquiries, sales or something more specific.
  • What changed recently, such as a redesign, migration, traffic drop, new competitor or new market.
  • Any evidence you already have from Google Search Console, Google Analytics or another analytics workflow.

You do not need a perfect diagnosis

It is completely fine to say, “traffic dropped and I do not know why,” “my pages are not ranking for the searches that matter,” or “I am not sure whether I need an audit or ongoing SEO.”

The important part is describing the situation honestly. The analysis can come after that.

A PRACTICAL, ETHICAL APPROACH

Clear advice matters as much as the tactic.

SEO should be useful to the people who search, the business that owns the site and the people responsible for implementing the work. That means avoiding tactics that rely on deception, manufactured signals, copied pages or promises that cannot be supported by the available evidence.

Recommendations should be explainable

You should be able to understand what is being changed, why it matters, how it relates to search behaviour and what will be measured afterward. A recommendation is more useful when the reasoning behind it is clear.

Results need the right expectations

SEO does not come with a universal timeline or guaranteed ranking position. Competition, site history, technical conditions, content, authority, market demand and implementation speed all influence what happens. The goal is to make the next decision better informed.
