BEFORE YOU COMMIT TO ONGOING SEO

There should be a clear reason for every recurring task.

Monthly SEO should not become a monthly checklist where the same reports, edits or link-building activities continue simply because they were part of a package. A useful subscription has a changing priority system. It reacts to search demand, technical conditions, published content, competitors, business changes and the evidence collected over time. That makes it easier to explain what is being done and why.

The right scope can also change. One month may require technical cleanup, another may need content development, and another may be focused on internal linking, local visibility, ecommerce structure or authority. The important part is that the work remains connected to the agreed objective rather than generating activity for its own sake.

A PRACTICAL & ETHICAL STANDARD

What I will not promise just to win a project.

Good marketing should not depend on making claims that cannot be defended. SEO has uncertainty because search engines, competitors, markets and websites change. That uncertainty should be explained rather than hidden.

No guaranteed rankings or timelines

A specific ranking position or universal timeline cannot be guaranteed responsibly. A realistic plan should identify the variables that can be influenced, the evidence that can be measured and the dependencies that may affect progress.

  • Recommendations are tied to the available evidence.
  • Priorities can change when new data appears.
  • Implementation quality matters as much as the recommendation itself.

No manufactured authority

Off-page SEO should not be reduced to collecting random links. Relevance, context and trust matter more than a large spreadsheet of low-value placements. The goal is to build a site that deserves to be referenced, not simply to manufacture signals.

  • Relevant references are more useful than irrelevant volume.
  • Link opportunities should be evaluated for quality and context.
  • Risky shortcuts can create more problems than they solve.

WHAT CAN START THE CONVERSATION

You can bring a problem, a question, or a plan.

Not every SEO enquiry begins with a clean brief. Sometimes a business owner knows that an important page is not getting the visibility it should. Sometimes a marketing team has a traffic chart that changed suddenly and needs help working out what the data actually says. Sometimes a new website, service, product line or market is about to launch and the main question is what should be prepared before the change goes live.

Common reasons people reach out

  • Organic traffic changed: You have noticed a drop, a plateau or an unusual change and want to distinguish a real SEO issue from seasonality, measurement differences or changes in search demand.
  • Important pages are not performing: A service, category, product or location page may be receiving impressions without meaningful clicks, or may not be appearing for the searches that match the business offer.
  • The site has technical questions: You may need to investigate crawling, indexing, redirects, canonicalization, JavaScript rendering, structured data, site speed or a migration before investing more heavily in content.
  • The content strategy feels unclear: You may have many topics but no clear keyword map, search-intent framework, internal-linking path or distinction between pages that should exist.
  • Local or ecommerce growth is the goal: The important question may involve location intent, Google Business Profile support, category structure, product pages, filters, variants or the customer journey from search to enquiry or purchase.
  • You are thinking about AI search: A team may want to understand how existing SEO foundations connect with AI-search visibility, answer-focused content and broader entity or information-architecture considerations without chasing every new acronym.

WHAT A CONSULTATION SHOULD FEEL LIKE

Clear questions first. Recommendations second.

A good consultation is not a performance where every answer is presented with certainty. There will be situations where the evidence is strong, situations where several explanations are possible, and situations where the right answer is to gather more information before changing the site.

The useful outcome is a clearer decision. That might mean identifying a technical issue that should be fixed before content work, narrowing a broad keyword target into a more realistic search-intent map, deciding which pages deserve improvement first, or recognizing that the website is not the main constraint at all. Recommendations should connect back to the business objective and to what can actually be implemented.

This is also why the contact form asks for your website and your primary SEO challenge rather than making you complete a long questionnaire. The form should collect enough context to start intelligently without making the first step feel like an application.

A useful starting principle: describe what you can observe, what you want to improve, and what changed. You do not need to turn that information into an SEO diagnosis before you send it.

From there, the relevant service can be chosen more confidently. A broader investigation may point toward an SEO Audit. A focused problem may fit SEO Consultation. Implementation work can then move into Technical SEO, On-Page SEO, Local SEO, Content SEO, Ecommerce SEO or AI Search SEO depending on the evidence.
