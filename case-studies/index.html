Case Studies Built on Evidence, Not Ranking Promises

I document SEO work with the source, time period, context and limitations visible. That includes my own website measurements, practical audits and authorized client evidence when it becomes available. Demonstration material stays separate from first-party results, and I do not publish invented traffic charts, fabricated reviews or unsupported ranking claims.

Proof over promises. The About page explains the wider principles behind that approach. This page shows how case-study evidence is collected, interpreted and presented so a reader can distinguish a finding from a performance claim.

How I Document a Case Study

A credible SEO case study should answer four questions: What was measured? Where did it come from? When was it measured? What does it actually prove? I keep those distinctions visible instead of turning one metric or one snapshot into a broader claim about rankings, traffic, leads or revenue.

When first-party or authorized evidence becomes available, the record can include the property or project, date range, metric definition, relevant implementation changes and known limitations. That makes the work easier to review, reproduce and understand in context.

What Counts as Evidence

  • First-party measurement: data from the website or an analytics property I control or am authorized to access.
  • Technical findings: observable issues and implementation details that can be checked against the website.
  • Authorized client evidence: project information, measurements or feedback shared with permission and enough context to interpret it responsibly.
  • Illustrative material: clearly labeled examples used to explain a reporting method, not to claim performance.

What does not count is invented metrics, anonymous success stories, copied screenshots, fabricated reviews, unsupported ranking claims or third-party data presented as my own.

How Performance Should Be Read

Search Console performance data and PageSpeed diagnostics answer different questions. Search performance should be read across an appropriate time period and search context, while page-performance diagnostics should be interpreted according to the measurement source rather than treated as one universal score.

For local SEO, business information and customer feedback should represent the real business accurately. Reviews should reflect genuine experiences rather than incentives or fabricated activity.

Trust note: Metrics are time-bound and context-dependent. Search Console clicks, Analytics sessions, engagement metrics and PageSpeed diagnostics measure different things. A performance snapshot is not a guarantee of rankings, traffic, leads or revenue.
