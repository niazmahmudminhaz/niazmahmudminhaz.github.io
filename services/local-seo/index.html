How local ranking works

Relevance, distance and prominence are useful starting points

Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. There is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. That makes the strategic job less about chasing a secret “Maps trick” and more about making the business representation accurate, relevant and useful while understanding the limits of geography and competition. citeturn366149search1

Relevance

How well the business information matches what the searcher is asking for. Complete and accurate information helps Google understand the business.

Distance

How far the business is from the searcher or the relevant location context. This is a factor no content strategy can simply write away.

Prominence

How well-known the business is in the relevant context. Google notes signals such as links and reviews can contribute to prominence.

What this changes in practice:

A local SEO strategy should not promise “#1 on Maps.” It should identify which parts of relevance, business representation, local authority, website usefulness or conversion experience are actually improvable, then measure those changes honestly.

Google Business Profile

Your Business Profile is an identity system, not a keyword box

Google's current guidance recommends keeping business information complete and accurate and explains that local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. citeturn366149search1 The SEO work is therefore about accurate representation first: who the business is, what it actually provides, where it genuinely serves customers, and how those facts line up with the website and other important references.

What I review

  • Business name and representation match the real business.
  • Primary and additional categories fit the actual business.
  • Services and business information are complete and consistent.
  • Website destination supports the same service and local intent.
  • Photos and media add genuine context instead of clutter.
  • Profile information does not claim a location or service the business does not actually have.

What I do not recommend

  • Stuffing city names or keywords into the business name.
  • Creating fake locations to appear closer to searchers.
  • Buying or manufacturing reviews.
  • Publishing misleading services or claims for extra visibility.
  • Building huge batches of weak directory profiles simply to create a number.
  • Treating a single Maps screenshot as the complete measurement system.

Accuracy is a ranking foundation and a trust foundation.

Local SEO works better when the business information a customer sees before a visit is the same information the business can actually stand behind.

Local landing pages

Location pages should earn their place

A location page should answer a real local need. It should make the service clear, explain the geographic relationship honestly, offer useful information and help a visitor decide what to do next. Creating near-identical pages with different city names is not a substitute for local relevance.

Local search intent

People search locally for different reasons

A mature local strategy does not treat every query containing a place name as the same opportunity. The page and action need to follow what the searcher is trying to accomplish.

Nearby service

“service near me”

Strong local intent. The visitor often needs a relevant business, proximity context and an easy action such as call, visit or request.

Service + place

“SEO consultant in Dhaka”

The visitor is evaluating a provider in a defined geography. A service-led page should explain capability first and geographic relevance clearly.

Problem + place

“emergency plumber Uttara”

The visitor has a specific need and location. The page needs immediate clarity about availability, service scope and the next action.

Comparison

“best dentist in Banani”

The user is evaluating options. Useful trust evidence, service detail and honest differentiation are more important than repeating the neighborhood name.

Local information

“dentist cost Dhaka”

The visitor needs information before choosing a provider. Educational content can support the journey and link naturally to the relevant service.

Brand / route

“business name Gulshan”

The task may be to verify identity, opening information, contact details or location. Accuracy becomes the priority.

Citations & entity consistency

Do not confuse “more listings” with better local authority

Citations are business references across directories and other sources. I treat them as part of an entity-consistency problem: important business details should be accurate where they are published, and the references should make sense for the business and market.

Useful citation work

Identify important industry, geographic and trusted directory sources. Correct outdated business details. Remove or address misleading duplicates where appropriate. Keep service information aligned with the actual business.

The objective is a cleaner business footprint that supports recognition and customer trust.

Low-value citation work

Submitting the same information to hundreds of weak directories because a tool reports a citation count can create busywork without solving a meaningful business problem.

Quality, accuracy and relevance are more useful filters than raw volume.

Reviews & trust

Reviews should reflect customer experience, not an SEO scheme

Reviews are primarily a customer trust asset. Google notes that prominence can include review-related signals, but the ethical process is straightforward: make it easy for real customers to share honest feedback, respond professionally and use the feedback to improve the service itself. citeturn366149search1

Ask normally

Invite genuine customers to leave a review through the platform's normal process without fabricating demand or forcing a positive outcome.

Respond usefully

Use replies to clarify facts, thank customers and demonstrate that the business pays attention to actual experience.

Learn from patterns

Recurring customer comments can reveal service, communication or operational issues that matter more than another directory listing.

Trust rule:

Do not manufacture testimonials, buy ratings or use misleading review tactics. Local SEO is not a reason to compromise the reputation of a real business.

Multi-location & service-area businesses

Geography needs to match reality

Multi-location businesses need clear separation between actual locations, services and customer journeys. Service-area businesses need equally clear geographic communication without pretending to have physical locations they do not have.

Multi-location brands

Build a structure where each genuine location can explain its own services, contact details, local context and next action while staying connected to the main brand architecture.

Service-area businesses

Represent the area served accurately. A service area is not permission to claim a storefront in every town you want to rank in.

Franchises

Keep national brand consistency while giving each legitimate location enough unique information to be useful to nearby customers.

Practitioners & professionals

Make service scope, real-world location, credentials and contact options clear without building pages that exist only for keyword variations.

FAQ

Questions worth answering before a Local SEO project starts

Good local strategy is built on real business conditions. These are the questions that usually clarify scope.

What does a Local SEO Consultant actually work on?

Local SEO can include Google Business Profile strategy, local keyword and intent research, local landing pages, on-page relevance, internal linking, business-information consistency, citation review, review-related guidance, local competitor research and measurement of local discovery and leads.

Is Google Business Profile enough for local SEO?

No. A Business Profile is an important local discovery asset, but local search also involves the website, service and location relevance, accurate business information, external references, customer trust and the competitive search landscape.

Do I need a separate page for every city or neighborhood?

Not automatically. A location page should have a genuine reason to exist and provide useful information for that place. Creating many near-identical pages that only swap city names can create a poor experience and may fall into doorway abuse.

Can local SEO guarantee a top Google Maps position?

No. Google states that local results are mainly influenced by relevance, distance and prominence, and there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

Should a business ask customers for reviews?

A business can build a normal review process that makes it easy for customers to share genuine experiences, provided the process follows the platform's rules and does not use fabricated, deceptive or manipulative reviews.

Is local SEO only for businesses with a storefront?

No. Service-area businesses can also need local search strategy, but their geographic messaging and Business Profile setup must accurately represent where the business serves customers rather than claiming locations where there is no genuine presence.
