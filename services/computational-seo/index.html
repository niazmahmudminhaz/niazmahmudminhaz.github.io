Core SEO Service

Computational SEO

Computational SEO turns large or repetitive SEO questions into measurable analysis. I use crawl data, Search Console evidence, structured datasets, lightweight scripts and repeatable checks to find patterns that are difficult to identify manually.

Best for: complex websites, large URL sets, technical investigations, internal-link analysis, scalable opportunity discovery and evidence-led prioritization.

What the Service Covers

Computational SEO can include URL inventory analysis, crawl-data inspection, indexing comparisons, internal-link graphs, template-level comparisons, title and heading pattern checks, status-code analysis and Search Console query/page relationships. The exact method depends on the question being answered.

From Data to Decisions

The objective is not to generate a spreadsheet for its own sake. Analysis should answer a useful business or search question. Examples include locating pages that receive impressions but lack appropriate internal links, finding repeated technical patterns across templates, comparing indexed and discovered URL groups, or identifying clusters where content and search demand do not align.

Typical Workflow

  1. Define the question, scope and decision the analysis needs to support.
  2. Collect suitable first-party data, crawl data or structured site data.
  3. Normalize and inspect the dataset for missing values, duplicates and misleading signals.
  4. Run targeted analysis and compare patterns against search intent and site architecture.
  5. Translate findings into prioritized SEO actions with clear assumptions and limitations.
  6. Validate the change and measure the relevant outcome rather than relying on rankings alone.

How It Connects to Other SEO Services

Computational SEO commonly supports SEO Audits, Technical SEO, Keyword Research, Content SEO and Competitive Analysis. Research on computational methods remains in Technical SEO Research, while practical explanations can appear in the Blog.

Evidence and Expectations

Methods are chosen to fit the available evidence. Computational analysis can reveal patterns and priorities, but it cannot guarantee rankings, traffic or indexing. Recommendations should be reproducible, documented and tied to a defined SEO decision.
