Competitive Analysis

Competitive SEO analysis identifies what competing pages are doing, where their strengths are relevant to your market, and where a realistic opportunity may exist. The goal is not to copy competitors. It is to understand the search landscape well enough to make better priorities.

Focus: SERP patterns, competitor page types, content gaps, information coverage, internal links, authority signals and strategic opportunities.

What I Compare

Depending on the project, I can compare target queries, ranking page types, page structures, topic coverage, internal-link patterns, titles and headings, content depth, business positioning and backlink or referring-domain patterns. Comparisons are interpreted in the context of your site rather than treated as a simple checklist.

Find Gaps, Not Just Winners

A competitor may rank because of factors that are difficult or inappropriate to replicate. Useful competitive research therefore looks for gaps: underserved search intents, unclear explanations, weak page architecture, missing supporting resources, poor internal links or areas where your business can provide better first-party evidence.

Typical Workflow

  1. Define the market, competitors and priority search areas.
  2. Collect representative SERP and competitor-page evidence.
  3. Group competitors by actual search intent and page type.
  4. Compare content, architecture, internal links and authority patterns.
  5. Identify realistic opportunities and separate them from low-value imitation.
  6. Prioritize actions against business impact, effort and evidence.

Deliverables

Outputs may include competitor sets, SERP observations, page-type comparisons, content-gap findings, topic opportunities, internal-link observations, authority comparisons and a prioritized action plan. The result should help decide what to improve, what to create and what not to chase.

Relationship to Other Services

Competitive Analysis supports Keyword Research, Search Intent Analysis, Content SEO, Backlink Analysis and SEO Audits. Research-driven analysis belongs in Insights; practical explanations belong in the Blog.

Evidence and Expectations

Competitive analysis is directional. Competitor visibility can be influenced by factors outside the dataset being reviewed, and search results change over time. The work supports informed decisions rather than promising a specific ranking outcome.
