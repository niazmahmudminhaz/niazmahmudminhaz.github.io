Research Lane 01

SEO Experiments & Findings

This research lane documents how SEO questions can be tested rather than treated as assumptions. The focus is on hypotheses, baselines, measurable changes, observations and limitations.

Research Framework

  1. Define the question and expected mechanism.
  2. Record a baseline and choose meaningful metrics.
  3. Change one relevant variable where practical.
  4. Observe over an appropriate period and compare against context.
  5. Document the finding, confidence and known limitations.

What Counts as a Useful Finding?

A useful finding does not need to be dramatic. A result can clarify that a change had little measurable effect, affected one template but not another, or worked only under specific conditions. Clear negative or uncertain results can prevent poor strategic decisions.

Measurement Matters

SEO experiments may use Search Console, analytics, crawl data, indexation observations, page-level metrics or controlled content comparisons. The metric should match the question. A ranking change alone may not explain why traffic, impressions, clicks or conversions changed.

From Research to Services

Commercial implementation remains under Services. Experiments may inform SEO Audits, Technical SEO, Content SEO and Computational SEO. Practical explanations can be published in the Blog.

Research Limits

Search systems change and many SEO variables cannot be perfectly isolated. Findings should therefore be presented with scope, evidence and uncertainty rather than universal claims.
