SEO Methodologies: Information Gain & Evidence

Information gain is a useful way to think about whether a page adds meaningful value to a search journey. This research lane examines how original evidence, synthesis, examples and clearer structure can improve usefulness.

Beyond Repetition

Covering the same facts as every competing page does not automatically make a resource useful. A stronger page may add first-party observations, better organization, original comparisons, practical examples, documented processes or a clearer explanation of trade-offs.

Evidence Sources

Useful evidence can include first-party data, documented experiments, direct observations, source material, product or service experience and carefully cited external research. Evidence should be relevant to the claim being made and its limitations should be clear.

Method for Evaluating Information Gain

  1. Define the user's information need.
  2. Review what existing high-quality resources already cover.
  3. Identify meaningful gaps, ambiguities or unanswered questions.
  4. Add evidence or synthesis that genuinely addresses those gaps.
  5. Structure the page so readers can verify and use the information.
  6. Review the page periodically as the topic and evidence change.

Connection to Content SEO

This methodology informs the commercial Content SEO service, but the research itself remains here. Broader practical explanations can appear in the Blog, while technical implementation is handled through the relevant Services.

Research Limits

Information gain is not a single measurable score that guarantees search performance. It is a reasoning framework for improving usefulness, originality and evidence.
